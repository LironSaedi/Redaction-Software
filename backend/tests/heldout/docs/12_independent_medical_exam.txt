RESPONSES TO COUNSEL'S QUESTIONS
Q1. Were the examinee's injuries caused by the collision of [[DATE|March 14, 2025]]?
    The left distal radius fracture, rib fractures, splenic laceration, and concussion are, in
    reasonable medical probability, caused by the collision. The cervical condition represents, in
    reasonable medical probability, an aggravation of a pre-existing degenerative condition at
    C5-C6. The 2021 study shows a pre-existing bulge; the 2025 study shows a larger protrusion with
    annular fissure. Annular fissures may be degenerative. I cannot state within reasonable
    medical probability that the collision caused the C5-C6 protrusion de novo; it more likely
    than not aggravated it.
Q2. Is the treatment to date reasonable and necessary?
    Yes, with the exception that 31 PT visits exceed ODG guidelines (16 visits over 8 weeks for
    distal radius fracture post-ORIF); visits after [[DATE|06/15/2025]] appear excessive.
Q3. Has the examinee reached maximum medical improvement?
    Not for the wrist. A wrist arthroscopy with TFCC debridement/repair and possible carpal tunnel
    release is reasonable. For the neck, she is near MMI with conservative care. I do not believe
    cervical surgery (ACDF) is reasonably probable at this time, although it is possible.
Q4. Future medical care?
    Wrist arthroscopy +/- CTR (~[[MONEY?|$18,000-24,000]] facility and professional); post-op therapy 12
    visits; hardware removal possible (25-30% probability). Cervical: medial branch blocks or ESI
    up to 2 per year for 2 years; home exercise. I disagree with the life care planner's projection
    of a C5-C6 ACDF at 100% probability.
Q5. Work restrictions?
    Temporary: no repetitive fine pinch with the left hand > 20 min/hr; no lifting > 15 lb with
    the left hand. Ms. [[PERSON|Delgado-Ruiz]] is right-hand dominant; a dental hygienist uses both hands
    extensively (left hand for mirror and retraction). Return to full duty is reasonably probable
    within 4-6 months after wrist arthroscopy.
Q6. Impairment (AMA Guides, 6th Ed.) -- provisional, not at MMI:
    Wrist: Class 1, grade modifier adjustments -> 6% UEI = 4% WPI. Cervical: Class 1 radiculopathy,

DISCUSSION
The literature I relied upon includes: Jupiter JB, Fernandez DL, "Complications following distal
radial fractures," J Bone Joint Surg Am 2001;83(8):1244-65; Carragee EJ et al., "Is cervical neck
pain and radiculopathy related to MRI findings?" Spine 2005; and the Official Disability
Guidelines (ODG), Neck and Upper Back chapter (2024). I am also aware of the Texas standard
for expert causation testimony discussed in Merrell Dow Pharmaceuticals, Inc. v. Havner, 953
S.W.2d 706 (Tex. 1997), and Guevara v. Ferrer, 247 S.W.3d 662 (Tex. 2007), as explained to me by
counsel; my opinions are medical, not legal.

The examinee was credible and gave consistent effort. Her grip testing was reproducible. There
were no signs of symptom magnification. Her description of the prior 2021 neck episode was
incomplete at first but she corrected it without prompting once reminded. Her husband stated
she "hasn't been the same" and that she no longer lifts their [[INDIRECT_ROLE?|youngest]] or braids their
daughter's hair. I note that [[MEDICAL_CONDITION|hypothyroidism]] is a recognized risk factor for carpal tunnel
syndrome; however, she had no carpal tunnel complaints before [[DATE|3/14/25]] and was euthyroid (TSH
2.84) during the admission.
