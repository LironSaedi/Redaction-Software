     8.  On the evening of [[DATE|July 4, 2025]], [[PERSON|Olumide Adeyemi]] -- husband, father, engineer, and church elder --
walked with his wife and their [[INDIRECT_ROLE|fifteen-year-old daughter]] from the Independence Day fireworks at
[[INDIRECT_LOCATION|Living Waters Parish on Fondren Road]] toward their car. At approximately 9:52 p.m., the family
entered the marked crosswalk across Westheimer Road at Fondren Road on a "WALK" signal.
     9.  At the same moment, Defendant [[PERSON|Pruitt]], driving a [[VEHICLE_DESCRIPTION|red 2021 Dodge Ram 1500]] (Texas license plate
[[LICENSE_PLATE|LXR4790]], VIN [[VIN|1C6SRFFT9MN220417]]) westbound on Westheimer, ran the red light at approximately 48 mph
in a 35-mph zone. [[PERSON|Olumide]] saw the truck, pushed his daughter backward out of its path, and was struck.
He was thrown approximately 70 feet. He died at [[ORGANIZATION?|Westchase General Hospital]] at 11:31 p.m., with his
wife holding his hand.

A blood specimen drawn under warrant at 12:40 a.m. on [[DATE|July 5, 2025]] measured 0.151 g/100 mL. [[PERSON|Pruitt]] [[CRIMINAL_HISTORY|was
previously convicted of Driving While Intoxicated in Fort Bend County in 2019]].
     11. In the four hours before the collision, [[PERSON|Pruitt]] was served at least nine 22-oz. draft beers and two
shots of whiskey at [[ORGANIZATION?|Sidelines]], according to his own bar tab (Visa ending [[CREDIT_CARD|3306]], opened 5:41 p.m., closed

                                  V. CAUSES OF ACTION
     18. Negligence and negligence per se -- Defendant [[PERSON|Pruitt]]. Defendant [[PERSON|Pruitt]] owed a duty to exercise
ordinary care. He breached that duty, and violated Tex. Transp. Code §§ 544.007 (traffic signals), 545.351
(speed), and 552.002 (pedestrian right-of-way), and Tex. Penal Code § 49.04 (DWI), each of which was
designed to protect pedestrians such as Decedent.
     19. Gross negligence -- Defendant [[PERSON|Pruitt]]. Driving at nearly twice the legal limit, speeding, through a red
light, into a crowded crosswalk on the Fourth of July, with a prior DWI conviction, involved an extreme degree
of risk of which he had actual, subjective awareness. Plaintiffs seek exemplary damages, which are not subject
to the statutory cap because the conduct constitutes a felony under Tex. Penal Code § 49.08. Tex. Civ. Prac.
& Rem. Code § 41.008(c)(10).
     20. Dram Shop Liability -- Defendant [[ORGANIZATION?|Sidelines]]. Under Tex. Alco. Bev. Code § 2.02(b), [[ORGANIZATION?|Sidelines]] is liable
because at the time it provided alcoholic beverages to [[PERSON|Pruitt]] it was apparent to the provider that he was
obviously intoxicated to the extent that he presented a clear danger to himself and others, and his
intoxication was a proximate cause of Decedent's death. See F.F.P. Operating Partners, L.P. v. Duenez, 237
S.W.3d 680 (Tex. 2007). The "safe harbor" of § 106.14 does not apply because [[ORGANIZATION?|Sidelines]] encouraged
overservice through its "Bottomless Fourth" promotion.
